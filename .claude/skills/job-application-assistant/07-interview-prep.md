# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Phantom Avenue organic growth (+30% traffic) (SEO / content)
**S:** Phantom Avenue Tattoo Shop needed stronger online discovery; organic traffic and local visibility were underperforming.
**T:** Own marketing as Manager/Marketing Manager — website, local SEO, content, and social.
**A:** Redesigned the website for mobile performance; built local SEO (citations, Google Business Profile); optimized content; ran trend-driven social with video and photography.
**R:** Organic traffic increased by 30%.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a marketing win", "How do you approach SEO?", "Describe a time you owned a channel end-to-end"

### 2. Multi-account SEO at Lumin (agency SEO / leadership)
**S:** As Co-founder and Marketing Director at Lumin Marketing Group, multiple client accounts needed rankings, traffic, and lead generation improvements.
**T:** Own SEO strategy and delivery across accounts while managing campaigns and budgets.
**A:** Ran keyword research, competitor analysis, and SERP tracking in SEMrush and Search Atlas; improved technical SEO, UX, and conversions on client sites; monitored performance and reported results.
**R:** Sustained multi-account SEO ownership with improved rankings, traffic, and lead-gen focus (cite specific client metrics in interview only if you have permission and numbers ready).
**Use for:** "How do you manage multiple clients?", "Walk me through your SEO process", "Describe agency experience"

### 3. Claude AI workflows for marketing ops (AI automation)
**S:** Client and internal marketing work included repetitive content, admin, CRM, and reporting tasks that slowed delivery.
**T:** Introduce practical AI automation using Claude without replacing human judgment on client-facing work.
**A:** Built Claude-powered workflows for marketing/ops tasks; connected automation to CRM/Go High Level where useful; documented SOPs; trained teams on safe use; used Claude Code for agentic tooling where relevant.
**R:** Reduced manual grunt work so the team could focus more on selling and client delivery. *Do not invent a specific hours-saved number until you quantify one.*
**Use for:** "How have you used AI at work?", "Tell me about automation you've built", "What's your AI philosophy?"

### 4. High-volume service operations at Audi (pace / customer ownership)
**S:** Audi dealership service environment with high customer volume and fast turnaround expectations.
**T:** As Service Manager, keep customers moving, protect relationships, and grow service revenue.
**A:** Managed high-volume interactions; communicated clearly; upsold recommended services; coordinated scheduling, documentation, and workflows.
**R:** Stronger client relationships and contribution to service revenue through structured upselling and ops coordination.
**Use for:** "Tell me about working under pressure", "How do you handle difficult customers?", "Describe operational ownership"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why are you looking / why leave Lumin (or take another role)?"
> I'm open to roles where I can apply marketing + practical AI automation at greater scale. I'm proud of what we've built at Lumin; I'm looking for the next place where ownership, pace, and AI-enabled marketing are core to the job — Edmonton or remote, at CAD $60k+.

### "You don't have a university degree."
> Correct — my path is portfolio and results based: multi-year SEO and digital marketing delivery, Google Ads and Claude certifications, and measurable outcomes like the 30% organic traffic lift at Phantom Avenue. I'm happy to walk through process, tools, and examples rather than credentials.

### "You don't have [software engineering / ML research] experience."
> My AI work is applied: Claude workflows, marketing/ops automation, CRM systems, and enablement — not model training or backend engineering. If the role needs that applied layer, I'm a strong fit; if it needs ML research or production software engineering, I'd be honest that it's not my core.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Leading or deeply owning AI-enabled marketing systems — SEO/growth plus automation — where I help teams ship faster and measure results. I want more sophisticated AI workflow design while staying close to customer and campaign outcomes.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> I default to high pace and long hours, which is a strength in agency life but can over-index on urgency. I mitigate by documenting SOPs and automations so quality doesn't depend on me grinding every hour.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the campaign/content lifecycle look like, from idea to live?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tools, AI & Growth
- "What's your current stack for SEO, ads, CRM, and analytics?"
- "How is the team using AI today, and where is that going?"
- "Is there room to own automation and workflow design, not just channel execution?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture and pace?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between new growth projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"
- Quantify AI time savings only when you have a real number

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
